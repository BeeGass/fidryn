    The figures on this page are those of the sample of the United States Code committed in this repository, under samples/uscode/, and of nothing else. The sample holds three whole titles of the release 119-142 of the official XML release point published by the Office of the Law Revision Counsel: Title 1, Title 11 Appendix and Title 45. The table below is generated from the coverage ledger of the sample's workspace, samples/uscode/workspace/coverage.json, which fidryn coverage prints and The coverage ledger describes key by key.

    9.1 What the words mean #

    The ledger says how far the provisions of the sample are taken in three words, and in no others.

    • Imported. The official text of the provision is in a module, read from a pinned file, the publisher's archive, whose size and digests the importer verifies against the retrieval record that says where and when it was fetched. The lock of the workspace records the digests of each archive and of each module file, and the check of the workspace verifies every module file against them.
    • Addressed. The provision has a typed address, and its place in the hierarchy, its status and the references in its text are resolved and checked. Every provision imported is addressed. Each reference has an outcome, and the table gives the references of each layer by outcome, so that a reader sees how many of them reach their target.
    • Classified. What kind of provision it is, is recorded. At this version the one kind that is recorded is a definition, and the ledger gives the number of definitions extracted, by basis and by kind; a definition with a condition, or with source words in the place of a link, is counted as any other. It gives no count of classified provisions, since one provision can hold more than one definition.

    9.2 The basis of a definition #

    Each definition extracted has one of three bases:

    • enacted: its reach names a region of the Code, and the file of its unit declares its title positive law, so the words that state the reach are those the law enacted.
    • restated: its reach names a region of the Code, and the file of its unit does not declare its title positive law, so the words are those of the editors of the Code, and the reach the law enacted may differ from the one they state.
    • unresolved: the rules found no region of the Code for it, for one of four causes: it states no reach that they read; its reach is a link to a law outside the Code, such as a public law; its reach words are in a form the rules do not resolve, whatever they name: a part of the Code, as "this chapter and chapter 5" and, of a court rule, "this Rule 3021" do, or a law outside it, as "this Act" does, unless those words are those of one link to an address of the Code, whose target is then the region; or its reach is in a form they resolve, "this" and the name of a kind of provision or container, but neither the provision that states the reach nor any provision or container above it is of that kind, as with "As used in this subtitle" in a section that stands in no subtitle.

    The rules read definitions written in a closed list of forms (Definitions), and they do not find every definition that the text holds. The forms they leave out are listed, with their counts measured on the whole of release point 119-142, in What stays out; the share they miss has not been measured for the sample.

    9.3 Counted and not imported #

    Some of what the publisher's files hold is by rule not imported. The importer counts it in the lock of the workspace under these six names, and the ledger copies the counts:

    • tables_of_contents: the tables of contents, whose entries repeat the headings of the units they list;
    • footnotes_in_tables_of_contents: the footnotes inside them;
    • links_in_tables_of_contents: the links inside them;
    • release_notes: the notes that say which release a file is current through;
    • links_without_words: the links that have no words, or white space alone;
    • note_group_headings: the cross headings that open the groups of notes, whose labels are not imported; what a cross heading holds after its label is imported, as a note of its group.
